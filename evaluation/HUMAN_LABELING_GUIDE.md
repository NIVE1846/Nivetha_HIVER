# Human Labeling Guide

This guide is for manual review of `evaluation/golden_set.csv`. The `suggested_intent` value was generated by ordered keyword rules. It is a hint only, not ground truth. Read the complete customer message before choosing `human_intent`.

Use the simple terminal workflow:

```bash
python evaluation/review_golden.py
```

The script loads the CSV, shows one example at a time, saves progress after each example,
and lets you resume later. It never automatically fills `human_intent` for you.

Use exactly one of these labels:

- `playback_issue`
- `app_bug`
- `account_login`
- `premium_billing`
- `download_offline`
- `content_search`
- `general_inquiry`
- `other`

## Labeling Rules

Choose the single intent that best describes the customer's primary problem. When two issues appear, choose the more urgent or action-driving issue and explain the secondary issue in `labeling_notes`. Use `labeling_notes` for ambiguity, missing context, or the reason you disagreed with the suggestion.

### playback_issue

**Definition:** Audio playback is stopping, skipping, buffering, freezing, shuffling incorrectly, or using repeat/crossfade incorrectly while Spotify is running.

**Use when:** The content is present but the listening experience is broken, such as songs stopping after each track, audio cutting out, buffering, or shuffle playing the wrong order.

**Do not use when:** The app itself crashes or will not open (`app_bug`); downloaded/offline content is missing or cannot sync (`download_offline`); the customer cannot find a song, album, artist, or playlist (`content_search`).

**Examples:** "music keeps buffering on wifi"; "shuffle turns off after the song changes"; "songs skip to the next track randomly".

**Confusing edge cases:** "app crashes when I press play" is `app_bug`; "downloaded songs won't play offline" is usually `download_offline`; "this song is not available" is `content_search` unless it was already playing and then failed.

### app_bug

**Definition:** The Spotify application or interface crashes, freezes, shows an error, will not open, or is broken after an update.

**Use when:** The app is unusable independently of a specific account, payment, download, or audio problem.

**Do not use when:** The failure is specifically login/account access (`account_login`), audio playback (`playback_issue`), or downloads/offline sync (`download_offline`).

**Examples:** "the app keeps crashing when I open it"; "I get error 404"; "the screen is blank after the update".

**Confusing edge cases:** A login-specific error is `account_login`; a song stopping while the app remains open is `playback_issue`; asking whether a future update is coming is `general_inquiry`.

### account_login

**Definition:** The customer cannot access or manage their account through login, password recovery, social login, or account security.

**Use when:** They cannot sign in, forgot a password, cannot receive a reset, are locked out, have a wrong account email, or have a Facebook/Google login problem.

**Do not use when:** The issue is payment/subscription (`premium_billing`) or the app crashes before login (`app_bug`). Informational questions about profile settings without an access problem are `general_inquiry`.

**Examples:** "I can't log in with Facebook"; "I forgot the email for my account"; "my account is locked".

**Confusing edge cases:** "I logged in but Premium is not active" is `premium_billing`; "how do I change my username?" is `general_inquiry` unless access is blocked.

### premium_billing

**Definition:** Money, Premium subscription status, payment, cancellation, refund, trial, or plan management is involved.

**Use when:** The customer reports a charge, double billing, payment failure, refund request, unwanted renewal, cancellation problem, or Premium features missing despite payment.

**Do not use when:** Premium is mentioned only as background to an app or playback bug. General questions about what Premium includes or costs are `general_inquiry` unless an account charge or subscription problem is reported.

**Examples:** "I was charged twice"; "I cancelled but I'm still billed"; "my Premium trial ended unexpectedly".

**Confusing edge cases:** "I'm Premium and the app crashes" is `app_bug`; "I want to cancel" is `premium_billing`; a country-specific content availability issue is usually `content_search` or `general_inquiry`.

### download_offline

**Definition:** Downloading music or playlists, offline mode, saved content, device sync, or download storage is not working.

**Use when:** Downloads disappear, fail, remain stuck, do not sync, or offline mode cannot access content.

**Do not use when:** Streaming audio is broken without an offline/download aspect (`playback_issue`), the app itself crashes (`app_bug`), or the customer is installing the application rather than downloading music.

**Examples:** "my downloaded songs disappeared"; "offline mode says there are no songs"; "the playlist won't sync to my phone".

**Confusing edge cases:** If downloaded files exist but audio playback is the only problem, use `playback_issue`; if downloads themselves are missing or corrupted, use `download_offline`.

### content_search

**Definition:** A specific song, album, artist, playlist, podcast, library item, or catalogue content cannot be found or is unavailable where expected.

**Use when:** Search results omit content, a playlist disappears, recently added content is absent, or content is unavailable in a region.

**Do not use when:** The customer found the content but it will not play (`playback_issue`), downloaded content is missing offline (`download_offline`), or they cannot find a settings control (`app_bug` or `general_inquiry`).

**Examples:** "I can't find this album on Spotify"; "my playlist disappeared"; "this artist is not available in my country".

**Confusing edge cases:** "Spotify is not available in my country" may be `general_inquiry` if it is a general availability question; use `content_search` when a particular catalogue item is unavailable.

### general_inquiry

**Definition:** A general how-to question, feature request, compatibility question, feedback, availability question, or clear Spotify question that does not fit a more specific problem intent.

**Use when:** The customer asks how a feature works, requests a feature, gives feedback, asks about device compatibility, or confirms resolution and no underlying issue can be identified.

**Do not use when:** A specific playback, app, account, billing, download, or content-search problem is described. Do not use it as the automatic answer to every uncertain message when `other` is more appropriate.

**Examples:** "Can you add a sleep timer?"; "Is Spotify available in India?"; "How do I change my username?"; "Thanks, it works now" when no underlying issue is available.

**Confusing edge cases:** A follow-up such as "still not working" should use the inferred underlying intent if the message provides context. If no context is available, use `other` rather than guessing a specific intent.

### other

**Definition:** The message cannot be responsibly assigned to any Spotify support intent from the available text.

**Use when:** It is off-topic, directed at another brand, spam, a standalone URL, or a context-free reply such as "yes", "ok", or "no" where the underlying issue cannot be inferred.

**Do not use when:** The message is a vague but clearly Spotify-related question or feedback (`general_inquiry`), or a short follow-up whose underlying issue is stated or recoverable from the message itself.

**Examples:** "yes"; "ok"; a URL with no text; an unrelated message about another service.

**Confusing edge cases:** "Thanks, that fixed it" can be `general_inquiry` if it is clearly a Spotify resolution confirmation; use `other` when it is impossible to tell what the message refers to. Do not avoid `other` merely because the message contains a Spotify keyword.

## Escalation Label

Set `should_escalate` to `YES` when a human should review the message before an automated support response is sent. Use `YES` for:

- Billing, payment, refund, subscription, or account-access/security cases (`premium_billing` and `account_login`).
- `other` messages, because intent and safe response cannot be determined.
- Any message whose ambiguity or missing context makes an automated response unsafe, especially when multiple high-impact issues are present.

Set `should_escalate` to `NO` when the intent is clear, the issue is a routine low-risk Spotify support problem, and a grounded response could be selected from matching evidence. This commonly includes clear playback, app-bug, download/offline, content-search, and general-inquiry cases.

Use the message as the basis for the decision, not the existing suggested escalation value. If you choose `YES` for a non-billing or non-account message, explain why in `labeling_notes`.

## Follow-up And Ambiguity

Many examples are conversational follow-ups. Infer the issue only when the message itself gives enough evidence. If context is missing, use `other` and write `follow-up context unclear` in `labeling_notes`.

Mark uncertainty or two equally plausible intents in `labeling_notes`, but still choose one `human_intent`. Never copy `suggested_intent` without independently reading the message.
